Cycle 6 of 8 · Weeks 31–36

Technology & AI

Used Well, Not Just Used

Strand: Digital & AI-Fluent Educator Foundation + Enhanced Tier ~5.5 contact hours

Learning Goals

  • Distinguish AI use that supports learning from AI use that replaces it.
  • Trial one classroom policy or routine that makes student thinking visible again.
  • Practice explaining an AI-related decision to students in plain, non-punitive language.

Seed Problems

Illustrations only — a teacher's own live problem always takes priority in the Problem Ledger.

  • Students are submitting AI-generated essays and the teacher is unsure how to respond pedagogically rather than punitively.
  • A teacher wants to use an AI tool for lesson planning but isn't sure where to draw the line on relying on it.

Global Practice Briefing

2025 reporting on AI in professional learning shows a consistent design pattern worth borrowing directly: tools like Edthena's AI Coach succeed not by replacing a teacher's judgement but by walking them through structured stages — analyse, reflect, enact, impact — on evidence the teacher already generated. The same logic applies to AI in the classroom. Process-visible writing — requiring drafts and in-class writing time — combined with repositioning AI as a Socratic questioning partner rather than an answer-generator, echoes a teaching method far older than any AI tool: the Socratic method's insistence that the value is in the question, not the answer supplied. This cycle's Global Practice Briefing is explicit that the goal is not detection or prohibition, which research on AI-generated writing shows is unreliable and often punitive in effect, but redesigning the task so the thinking has to happen visibly, in the room, regardless of what tools exist outside it.

Cycle 6 · Technology & AI
Problem
Students are submitting AI-generated essays; the teacher is unsure how to respond pedagogically rather than punitively.
Context
Grade 9, English, one section piloted, Enhanced Tier
What was tried
In-class draft checkpoints plus an AI-permitted-for-brainstorming-only policy, explained to students with reasoning rather than as a rule.
What happened
Essay quality, by the teacher's own rubric, improved in the trial section relative to a comparison section; sample size was small and the result is reported with that caveat attached.
