Cycle 4 of 8 · Weeks 19–24

Assessment & Feedback

Feedback That Actually Moves Learning

Strand: Reflective Practitioner Foundation + Enhanced Tier ~5.5 contact hours

Learning Goals

  • Replace at least one piece of vague or grade-only feedback with task-, process-, or self-regulation-level feedback.
  • Build a lightweight formative check into an existing routine, not as extra work.
  • Notice the difference between feedback students act on and feedback they ignore.

Seed Problems

Illustrations only — a teacher's own live problem always takes priority in the Problem Ledger.

  • Students glance at the grade on returned work and never read the comments underneath it.
  • A teacher can't tell, in the moment, which half of the class actually understood today's lesson.

Global Practice Briefing

The research on feedback is unusually consistent: work following Hattie and Timperley's framework finds feedback only moves learning when it is timely, specific, and tied to a clear standard the student understands — and that feedback focused on grades, rewards, or personal traits has a low or even negative effect on motivation. A useful distinction for this cycle is the three levels: task feedback (was this correct), process feedback (how could the method improve), and self-regulation feedback (what would you check yourself, next time). Most classroom feedback stays stuck at the task level, in the form of a grade or a checkmark. Recent research on graduate and school settings also stresses timing: feedback that arrives after a student has mentally closed the loop on a task is far less likely to be read, let alone acted on. This cycle's Global Practice Briefing focuses on making the shift to process- and self-regulation-level feedback without adding grading time — often by asking the student to generate the feedback themselves against a clear standard, rather than the teacher writing it for them.

Cycle 4 · Assessment & Feedback
Problem
Students read the grade on returned essays and skip the written feedback entirely.
Context
Grade 8, English, class of 32, Enhanced Tier
What was tried
Grades withheld for one week; students first responded in writing to two process-level questions before receiving the numeric grade.
What happened
Written responses showed specific engagement with feedback for 26 of 32 students, a marked rise from a prior spot-check where almost none could recall their last piece of feedback.
