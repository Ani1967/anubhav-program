Cycle 1 of 8 · Weeks 1–6

Foundations

Building the Room: Culture, Trust, and the Loop Itself

Strand: Collaborative Co-Researcher Foundation + Enhanced Tier ~5.5 contact hours

Learning Goals

  • Establish the psychological-safety norms the whole program depends on.
  • Practice the four phases of the Anubhav Loop once, on a low-stakes problem, before real problems enter the Ledger.
  • Form buddy pairs and review groups that will stay together for the year.

Seed Problems

Illustrations only — a teacher's own live problem always takes priority in the Problem Ledger.

  • A new class doesn't yet know how to disagree with each other productively during discussion.
  • Teachers don't know their students' names and starting points well enough by week 3.

Global Practice Briefing

Every professional-learning system depends on psychological safety before it depends on technique — Etienne Wenger's communities-of-practice research is explicit that a shared domain and sustained relationships have to exist before a community can do useful joint work. Cycle 1 borrows two things to build that foundation fast. First, from lesson study's culture: the room normalises watching real, unpolished teaching together from day one, so "my classroom isn't perfect yet" is the baseline expectation, not a confession. Second, from the guru-shishya tradition's sambhasha (structured discussion) stage: disagreement is framed as a form of respect, not disruption, and every check-in opens with a stated norm rather than an assumed one. This cycle also runs the Loop once end-to-end on a deliberately low-stakes problem — a seating chart, a homework-return routine — so teachers experience Name, Try, Capture, and Return before anything they care deeply about is on the table. Getting the mechanics comfortable early is what andragogy would predict is necessary: adults need to understand why a structure exists before they'll trust it with something that matters.

Cycle 1 · Foundations
Problem
Grade 6 students shut down rather than respectfully disagree during class discussion.
Context
Grade 6, mixed subject, class of 34, Foundation Tier
What was tried
A 4-step "disagree well" sentence-starter card, taped to each desk, used for two weeks of discussion routines.
What happened
By week 3, unprompted use of sentence starters observed in roughly half the class; fewer discussions stalled into silence.
