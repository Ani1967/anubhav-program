Cycle 7 of 8 · Weeks 37–42

Inclusion & Differentiation

Designing for Variability From the Start

Strand: Adaptive Problem-Solver Foundation + Enhanced Tier ~5.5 contact hours

Learning Goals

  • Redesign one task using a Universal Design for Learning lens, rather than reacting case-by-case.
  • Distinguish anticipating learner variability (UDL) from reacting to it after the fact (traditional differentiation).
  • Trial a change that helps more than one student at once, not a single individualised fix.

Seed Problems

Illustrations only — a teacher's own live problem always takes priority in the Problem Ledger.

  • A teacher is making one-off accommodations for individual students reactively, and it's becoming unsustainable.
  • A student with limited English proficiency and a student with a reading difficulty both struggle with the same worksheet, for different reasons.

Global Practice Briefing

Universal Design for Learning research draws a sharp and useful distinction from traditional differentiated instruction: differentiation typically reacts to individual student needs one at a time, while UDL anticipates variability across an entire class and builds multiple means of engagement, representation, and expression into a task from the start. This is not a marginal difference in effect size — one controlled comparison found students taught with UDL principles were engaged for 44 of 60 minutes, against 19 minutes in a matched control class. The same research base is clear that implementation depends heavily on professional development and a supportive structure, not individual willpower; class size, physical resources, and organisational backing all shape how much flexibility a teacher can actually offer. This cycle's Global Practice Briefing asks teachers to pick one recurring task — a worksheet, a reading, a way of demonstrating understanding — and redesign it once with multiple entry points built in, rather than creating a new one-off accommodation every time a new need appears.

Problem
A teacher is making one-off accommodations for individual students reactively, and it has become unsustainable.
Context
Grade 6, Science, class of 36, Foundation Tier
What was tried
One lab-report task redesigned with three ways to demonstrate understanding: written report, oral explanation recorded as a voice note, or an annotated diagram.
What happened
Fourteen students chose a non-written option; two students who had previously needed individual accommodation completed the task independently for the first time this term.
