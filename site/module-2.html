Cycle 2 of 8 · Weeks 7–12

Engagement & Motivation

Reaching Reluctant and Disengaged Learners

Strand: Adaptive Problem-Solver Foundation + Enhanced Tier ~5.5 contact hours

Learning Goals

  • Diagnose disengagement as a solvable design problem rather than a fixed trait of a student.
  • Trial at least one choice-based or peer-structured engagement change.
  • Practice noticing and naming small wins in student participation.

Seed Problems

Illustrations only — a teacher's own live problem always takes priority in the Problem Ledger.

  • Six students refuse to read aloud, and two are meaningfully behind grade level.
  • A capable student does the bare minimum and disengages the moment work gets easy.

Global Practice Briefing

Active learning is one of the seven features Darling-Hammond's review of 35 rigorous studies found separates effective professional development — and the same principle applies directly to student engagement: students disengage fastest from tasks with no choice and no visible relevance. Two moves recur across the practices reviewed for this program. Paired and choice-based reading, which gives a struggling reader control over what they read while keeping the social scaffolding of a partner, shows consistent gains in willingness to read aloud. And near-peer tutoring — a stronger student teaching a specific skill to another — draws directly on the guru-shishya principle that teaching itself deepens the teacher's own understanding, a mechanism now well supported by cognitive-science research on the "protégé effect." Neither move requires new materials or technology; both require the teacher to deliberately hand over a small piece of control. This cycle's Global Practice Briefing focuses on identifying the smallest viable handover of choice or peer-responsibility a teacher can trial in their own room within four weeks.

Illustrative Practice Card

One of the three fully worked examples from the program design document.

Cycle 2 · Engagement & Motivation
Problem
Six students refuse to read aloud in a mixed-ability Grade 4 class; two are meaningfully behind grade level.
Context
Grade 4, Literacy, class of 30, Foundation Tier
What was tried
Paired-reading blocks three times a week for four weeks, each pair choosing from three levelled texts.
What happened
Four of six students voluntarily read aloud by week four; the remaining two read to their partner only, which the teacher counted as real progress.
Author
Illustrative exemplar (Worked Example 1 from the program design document)
Tags
Grade 3–5, Literacy, Low-tech
Lineage
Paired reading + choice-based text selection; near-peer tutoring in the spirit of guru-shishya mentorship.
