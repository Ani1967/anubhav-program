Cycle 3 of 8 · Weeks 13–18

Core Pedagogy

Active Learning in Mixed-Ability Classrooms

Strand: Reflective Practitioner Foundation + Enhanced Tier ~5.5 contact hours

Learning Goals

  • Move at least one routine from whole-class lecture toward active, structured practice.
  • Design a station or rotation model that keeps fast finishers and strugglers both productively occupied.
  • Use one piece of real classroom data (an exit ticket, a quick check) to inform grouping.

Seed Problems

Illustrations only — a teacher's own live problem always takes priority in the Problem Ledger.

  • A single teacher with 40 mixed-ability students has fast finishers bored and strugglers left behind, with no assistant support.
  • A lecture-heavy routine leaves the back third of the room visibly checked out by the 20-minute mark.

Global Practice Briefing

Lesson study's central discipline is treating a real, observable lesson as the object of collective improvement rather than talking about teaching in the abstract — and that discipline is most useful exactly where mixed-ability pressure is highest. Two well-evidenced structures recur: station rotation, which lets a teacher run differentiated tasks in parallel without needing additional staff, and a light, fast use of exit-ticket data to decide who goes where, rather than assuming yesterday's grouping still holds. Darling-Hammond's review again applies directly here: professional development that models effective practice concretely, rather than describing it, produces bigger shifts in classroom behaviour. This cycle's Global Practice Briefing therefore leans on short video and worked examples of station design rather than a lecture about differentiation theory, and treats a partially successful rotation as a legitimate, useful Cycle 3 outcome — a redesign candidate for the next Problem Ledger, not a failure to hide.

Illustrative Practice Card

Cycle 3 · Core Pedagogy
Problem
A 40-student mixed-ability maths class has fast finishers bored and strugglers left behind, with no teaching-assistant support.
Context
Grade 7, Mathematics, class of 40, Foundation Tier
What was tried
A three-station rotation twice weekly: guided practice with the teacher, peer-tutor-led review, and independent extension work, regrouped weekly using exit-ticket results.
What happened
Guided and peer-tutor stations ran well; the independent-extension station was chaotic and is flagged back into the Cycle 3 Problem Ledger for redesign rather than dropped.
Author
Illustrative exemplar (Worked Example 2 from the program design document)
Tags
Grade 6–8, Mathematics, Low-tech
Lineage
Station rotation + exit-ticket grouping + near-peer tutoring rota.
